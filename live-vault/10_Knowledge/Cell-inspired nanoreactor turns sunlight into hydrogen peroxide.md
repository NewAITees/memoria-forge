# Cell-Inspired Nanoreactor Turns Sunlight into Hydrogen Peroxide

## Overview
A new cell-inspired nanoreactor has been developed that uses sunlight to produce hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂). This nanoreactor mimics features of living cells to enhance the efficiency of light-driven chemical reactions, offering potential applications in cleaner chemical manufacturing and artificial photosynthesis.

## Details
The nanoreactor is a hollow CdS@polydopamine structure designed to mimic two key features of biological cells:

1. **Dynamic Catechol/o-Benzoquinone Redox Pair**: Located in the polydopamine shell, this pair acts as a proton relay rather than a proton pump, accelerating proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) processes.

2. **Compartmentalized Structure**: The nanoreactor features a nanoscale hollow cavity surrounded by a porous shell, creating a confined environment for reactants and photons, which enhances light-driven reactions.

Under visible-light illumination in an aqueous solution, the nanoreactor achieved an H₂O₂ photosynthesis rate of 3.24 mmol gcat⁻¹ h⁻¹ and a solar-to-chemical conversion efficiency of 1.2%.

The research was led by Prof. LI Can at the Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics (DICP) of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), in collaboration with Prof. Jian Liu's team at Inner Mongolia University. The findings were published in the *Journal of the American Chemical Society*.

The team also incorporated the nanoreactors into an environmentally friendly sodium alginate hydrogel matrix, creating solid and recyclable photocatalysts capable of continuous H₂O₂ production under natural sunlight.
