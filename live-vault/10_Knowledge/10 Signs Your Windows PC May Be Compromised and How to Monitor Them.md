# 10 Signs Your Windows PC May Be Compromised and How to Monitor Them

## Overview
Windows PCs run many background processes, which are generally safe. However, unusual processes or behaviors can indicate potential security threats. This article outlines 10 signs that your PC may be compromised and provides guidance on monitoring these issues.

## Signs Your PC May Be Compromised

1. **Unrecognized Processes**: Unknown processes running in Task Manager, especially from unusual locations like `C:\Users\AppData\Local\`, may indicate malware.
2. **High CPU Usage**: Unexpected high CPU usage when no active tasks are running can signal background processes consuming resources.
3. **Unknown Startup Applications**: New startup programs appearing without explanation may be malicious.
4. **Unusual Network Activity**: Unknown connections or unexpected outbound traffic could indicate data exfiltration.
5. **USB Device Activity**: Unexpected USB device connections may signal potential monitoring or data extraction.
6. **Changes to System Settings**: Unexpected modifications to system configurations, such as firewall settings or user permissions, should be investigated.
7. **Malware-Related Files in Temp Folders**: Unknown executable files in temporary folders like `C:\Users\User\AppData\Local\Temp\` may be malicious.
8. **Unexplained System Slowness**: A slow PC may be due to background processes consuming resources, even if not malware.
9. **PowerShell Usage**: While PowerShell is a legitimate tool, unusual or unexpected use may indicate malicious activity.
10. **Monitoring Tools**: Tools like SysPulse can help monitor system activity and provide alerts for new processes or changes.

## Monitoring and Investigating

### Tools for Monitoring
- **Task Manager**: Use `Ctrl + Shift + Esc` to check running processes and startup programs.
- **SysPulse**: A lightweight Windows security monitor that provides real-time alerts for new processes and system changes.
- **Windows Defender**: Regularly check for unexpected changes and ensure the antivirus is up to date.

### Investigative Steps
- **Check for Unknown Processes**: Look for processes with unfamiliar names or locations.
- **Review Startup Programs**: Use Task Manager to check for suspicious programs launching at startup.
- **Monitor USB Activity**: Track unexpected USB device connections.
- **Check System Logs**: Use Event Viewer to identify unusual system events.
